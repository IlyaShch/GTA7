## Inspiration
The release date for GTA 6 is getting closer. The theme “Royalty” made us wonder: what if the king was the person you stole? Our idea started with a runaway knight stealing a horse and evolved into a medieval kidnapping gone wrong.
 
## What it does
Grand Theft of A Horse is a 2D medieval chase game where you escape with a kidnapped king who refuses to stay quiet. During the chase, players need to be able to handle unexpected situations that arise suddenly.

## How we built it
We built the game in Godot using GDScript and collaborated through GitHub. We organized the game into separate scenes for the main menu, stable, and chase, using sprite sheets for animation and moving backgrounds to create the feeling of escape.

For the memory challenge, we track the sequence of card flips and compare the player’s selections against that order. We also used LMMS to create music for the game.

## Challenges we ran into
Learning to use Godot under the intense deadline pressure of a hackathon was a massive challenge. We leveraged AI to refine our code and overcome a series of hurdles, including node-script compatibility, scene switching, animation setup, and code integration via GitHub.

Additionally, we adjusted the challenge mechanics, shifting from the original "timed typing" mode to a "memory-based card matching" format. This required us to rethink the interaction design and clearly signal to players when to observe the sequence and when to take action.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of
We’re proud of giving the game a distinct personality through its unusual premise, playful visuals, and humor. We brought together programming, artwork, animation, and music while learning new tools.

We’re also proud that we were willing to rethink the gameplay and adapt our plans during development.

## What we learned
We learned how to connect scenes, signals, animations, and user input in Godot. Building the card challenge helped us understand how to manage different game states, such as showing a sequence, accepting input, and checking the result.

We also learned the importance of communication, version control, and keeping the project’s scope realistic under a tight deadline.

## What's next for Grand Theft of A Horse
We want to polish the card animations, improve visual feedback, and balance the difficulty with progressively longer sequences. We also want to improve scene transitions and background continuity.

Future additions could include more royal dialogue, different escape routes, and multiple endings—giving players more reasons to return and the king more reasons to complain.
